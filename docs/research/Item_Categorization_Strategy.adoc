## Item Categorization Strategy

### Goal

Design an effective and intuitive way to categorize lost and found items to improve searchability, reporting, and user experience within a campus-wide system.

### Description

Lost and found systems face a classification challenge similar to those found in e-commerce and inventory management platforms. Items must be categorized in a way that balances simplicity for users submitting items and precision for users searching them.

This section examines how common systems structure categories, compares flat and hierarchical approaches, and proposes a category structure suitable for a campus lost and found system.

### Categorization Approaches

#### Approach 1: Flat Categories

In a flat categorization system, all categories exist at a single level with no parent-child relationships.

**Example:**

- Electronics  
- Clothing  
- Accessories  
- Documents  
- Personal Items  
- Bags  

**Advantages**
- Simple to implement and maintain  
- Easy for users to understand  
- Faster item submission  

**Disadvantages**
- Limited precision when searching  
- Categories may become too broad  
- Difficult to scale as item variety grows  

#### Approach 2: Hierarchical Categories

Hierarchical categorization organizes items into parent categories with nested subcategories.

**Example:**

- Electronics  
  - Phones  
  - Laptops  
  - Chargers  
- Clothing  
  - Jackets  
  - Shirts  
  - Hats  

**Advantages**
- More precise classification  
- Improves filtering and search accuracy  
- Scales well as the system grows  

**Disadvantages**
- Slightly more complex to design  
- Requires clearer guidance during item submission  
- Deeper hierarchies may overwhelm some users  

#### Approach 3: Hybrid (Hierarchical + Attributes)

This approach combines a shallow hierarchy with descriptive attributes (tags or item properties).

**Example:**

- Category: Electronics > Phone  
- Attributes:
  - Brand  
  - Color  
  - Condition  
  - Location Found  

**Advantages**
- High flexibility and accuracy  
- Supports advanced filtering  
- Avoids overly deep category trees  

**Disadvantages**
- More complex data model  
- Requires consistent attribute definitions  

### Proposed Category Structure

A shallow hierarchical structure is recommended for a campus lost and found system, as it balances usability and precision.

**Top-level categories:**

- Electronics  
- Clothing & Accessories  
- Bags & Containers  
- Documents & IDs  
- Keys  
- Personal Items  
- Miscellaneous

### Conclusion

Compared to a flat category system, a shallow hierarchical approach provides better organization and search accuracy without introducing excessive complexity. This structure supports both efficient item submission and effective item discovery, making it well-suited for a campus lost and found application.
