= Lost & Found System – Identity & Anonymity Policy Research
Author: System Policy Proposal
Date: 2026-02-26
:toc:
:sectnums:

== 1. Objective

Determine whether the Lost & Found system should allow anonymous reports and define the minimum identity requirements for users performing the following actions:

* Reporting a Lost Item
* Reporting a Found Item
* Claiming an Item

The goal is to balance privacy, user participation, accountability, and fraud prevention.

== 2. Privacy vs. Accountability Tradeoff

=== Privacy Benefits
* Encourages higher participation.
* Reduces fear of retaliation or social exposure.
* Supports users uncomfortable sharing personal information publicly.
* Aligns with modern data-minimization practices.

=== Accountability Benefits
* Reduces fraudulent claims.
* Enables verification of item ownership.
* Prevents abuse and spam.
* Increases trust in the system.
* Allows moderation and dispute resolution.

=== Key Risk Areas
* False claims of ownership.
* Fake lost or found reports.
* Harassment through anonymous messaging.
* Legal liability if high-value items are mishandled.

A well-designed system should minimize identity exposure publicly while maintaining backend accountability.

== 3. Review of Similar Campus Systems

Most university Lost & Found systems follow these patterns:

1. Authentication Required (via student ID or institutional login).
2. Public anonymity allowed (name not shown publicly).
3. Identity verification required when claiming items.
4. Administrative oversight for dispute handling.

Examples (general patterns observed):
* Universities often require login through institutional email.
* Public listings typically hide personal contact details.
* Claimants must verify identity and describe the item accurately.
* High-value items require in-person ID verification.

Conclusion: Full anonymity is rarely allowed. Controlled or partial anonymity is common.

== 4. Policy Recommendation

=== Final Recommendation: PARTIALLY ALLOW ANONYMITY

Allow anonymity at the public display level, but require verified identity in the backend for all actions.

This balances privacy and accountability.

== 5. Identity Requirements by Action

=== 5.1 Reporting a Lost Item

* Minimum Requirement:
  - Verified campus login (student/staff account).
  - Institutional email required.
* Public Display:
  - Name hidden by default.
  - Optional display name (first name only or username).

* Justification:
  - Prevents spam and fake reports.
  - Allows administrators to contact the reporter.
  - Protects user privacy from public exposure.

Policy Decision: Allow public anonymity, require verified backend identity.

=== 5.2 Reporting a Found Item

* Minimum Requirement:
  - Verified campus login.
  - Institutional email required.
* Public Display:
  - Reporter identity hidden.
  - Messaging routed through internal system.

* Justification:
  - Prevents malicious or fake listings.
  - Protects finder from harassment.
  - Maintains traceability for item custody.

Policy Decision: Allow public anonymity, require verified backend identity.

=== 5.3 Claiming an Item

* Minimum Requirement:
  - Verified campus login.
  - Must provide:
    * Detailed description of item
    * Distinguishing features
    * Approximate date and location lost
  - For high-value items: in-person ID verification required.

* Public Display:
  - Claimant identity never public.

* Justification:
  - Highest fraud risk occurs during claim.
  - Protects rightful owners.
  - Reduces false claims.
  - Ensures institutional compliance and liability protection.

Policy Decision: Disallow anonymous claims. Full identity verification required.

== 6. Summary of Identity Policy

| Report Lost

== 7. Risk Mitigation Measures

* Internal messaging system (no direct email exposure).
* Admin moderation dashboard.
* Rate limiting to prevent spam.
* Audit logs for all claims.
* Escalation protocol for disputes.
* Automatic flagging of high-value items.

== 8. Final Justification

This partial anonymity model:

* Encourages user participation.
* Protects student privacy.
* Maintains institutional accountability.
* Reduces fraud risk.
* Aligns with common university best practices.
* Supports dispute resolution and administrative oversight.

Full anonymity would increase fraud risk and reduce system trust.
Full identity exposure would reduce participation and create privacy concerns.

Therefore, a controlled, authenticated-but-publicly-anonymous system is the optimal balance.

== 9. Final Decision

Anonymity Policy: PARTIALLY ALLOW

* Public anonymity: Allowed
* Backend identity verification: Required for all actions
* Claim identity verification: Mandatory and strict

This approach maximizes trust, usability, and security.
