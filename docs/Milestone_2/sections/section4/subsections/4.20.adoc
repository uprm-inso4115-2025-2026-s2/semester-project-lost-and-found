==== Domain Concept Formation and Abstraction
:author: Abdiel Cotto

===== Explanation of Concept Formation and Abstraction

Concept formation is the process of taking many concrete domain elements and grouping them into a smaller set of higher-level concepts that better explain how the domain works. Abstraction helps reduce noise by moving from individual details, such as `lost report`, `found report`, `status`, or `notification`, toward broader ideas that organize those details into meaningful structures.

In the Lost & Found domain, abstraction is useful because the system contains many repeated low-level elements that participate in the same overall process. By grouping them into concepts, the team can reason about responsibilities, relationships, and boundaries more clearly.

===== Low-Level Domain Elements Identified

The following low-level domain elements appear throughout the current documentation:

- Lost item
- Found item
- Report
- Report status
- Owner
- Finder
- Claimant
- Administrator or custodian
- Search query
- Filter by category, location, date, and status
- Matching
- Claim verification

- Private verification details

===== Grouping Elements into Higher-Level Concepts

[cols="2,4,5",options="header"]
|===
|Concept
|Grouped low-level elements
|Definition and role in the system

|Tracking Entities
|Lost item, found item, report, report status
|Tracking Entities are the domain objects whose state must be recorded and observed over time. They make loss and discovery visible by turning real-world item situations into structured records that can be searched, updated, and closed.

|Participants and Responsibilities
|Owner, finder, claimant, administrator, custodian
|Participants and Responsibilities capture the human roles involved in the domain. This concept clarifies who loses items, who reports discovered items, who attempts to reclaim them, and who oversees trust, moderation, or dispute handling.

|Discovery and Comparison
|Search query, filters, matching
|Discovery and Comparison describes how users move from many visible reports to a plausible candidate item. Its role is to reduce uncertainty by supporting search, narrowing results, and comparing report details for possible matches.

|Trust and Recovery Control
|Claim verification, private verification details, institutional email contact, recovery
|Trust and Recovery Control represents the safeguards that make return possible without exposing unnecessary personal information. Its role is to support legitimate recovery while reducing false claims, misuse, and unsafe communication.

|Lifecycle Governance
|Report status, closure, recovery, administrator or custodian actions
|Lifecycle Governance describes how reports change state from active use to resolved or closed outcomes. Its role is to keep the domain orderly, distinguish open cases from completed ones, and preserve confidence in the process.
|===

===== Concept Narrative

Within the UPRM Lost & Found domain, *Participants and Responsibilities* interact with *Tracking Entities* to make otherwise invisible loss incidents observable. A person who loses or finds an item creates or depends on a report, and those reports then move through *Discovery and Comparison* as users search, filter, and evaluate possible matches. Once a plausible match is identified, *Trust and Recovery Control* governs how the involved parties communicate and verify ownership safely. Finally, *Lifecycle Governance* ensures that each case reaches a clear outcome such as recovery, resolution, or closure so the domain remains understandable and manageable over time.

===== Inconsistency or Ambiguity in Current Descriptions

One important ambiguity in the current documentation is the role of communication. Some descriptions state that communication between parties occurs outside the system through official institutional email accounts, while other descriptions discuss system-supported messaging or internal secure messaging. These two descriptions imply different domain boundaries.

If communication is outside the system, then institutional email acts as an external coordination mechanism and should not be modeled as an internal platform service. If communication is inside the system, then messaging becomes part of the platform domain and requires clearer concepts for message records, privacy controls, and moderation. This ambiguity should be resolved so the domain narrative, requirements, and implementation all use the same abstraction boundary.

===== Why This Abstraction Improves Conceptual Clarity

This abstraction improves clarity because it replaces a flat list of terms with a small set of coherent conceptual groups. Instead of reasoning separately about every field, actor, and event, the team can discuss the domain in terms of stable concepts that explain purpose and interaction. That makes later requirements engineering easier because each requirement can be attached to a concept instead of being treated as an isolated detail.
