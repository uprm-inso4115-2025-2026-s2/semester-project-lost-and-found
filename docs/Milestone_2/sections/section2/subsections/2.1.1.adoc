==== Lost Item Owners (Students / Staff)

*Student (Mateo, freshman, engineering)*:

“I left my charger in a classroom. I realized at night.  
I didn’t know who to ask.  
I checked a couple of group chats, but nobody replied.”

- -> Observation: Loss is often discovered hours later, outside administrative hours.
- -> Derived concepts: _delayed realization_, _informal search attempt_, _peer-network reliance_, _after-hours uncertainty_, _ad-hoc communication_.


*Graduate Student (Luis, 34)*:

“I lost my keys somewhere between the parking lot and the business building.  
I wasn’t sure if I should check security, the library, or just retrace my steps.  
There’s no clear starting point.”

- -> Observation: No standardized entry point for recovery process.
- -> Derived concepts: _search ambiguity_, _recovery path uncertainty_, _multi-location probability_, _process opacity_, _spatial retracing_.


*Undergraduate (Valeria, lab assistant)*:

“My ID card fell out of my pocket once.  
I canceled it immediately because I was scared someone would misuse it.  
Later I found out someone had turned it in to the library.”

- -> Observation: Defensive actions taken before confirming recovery.
- -> Derived concepts: _preventive cancellation_, _identity risk perception_, _premature replacement_, _information delay_, _security anxiety_.


*Student (Anonymous quote overheard in hallway)*:

“I posted on Instagram that I lost my AirPods.  
Someone replied saying they found some, but it wasn’t mine.”

- -> Observation: Social media used as informal lost-and-found channel.
- -> Derived concepts: _platform fragmentation_, _false positive match_, _public broadcast attempt_, _unverified claim_, _informal matching_.


*Exchange Student (Sofia, 20)*:

“I don’t know where the lost and found office is.  
Back home, there’s always one central place.”

- -> Observation: Assumption of centralized infrastructure that may not exist.
- -> Derived concepts: _centralization expectation_, _physical office dependency_, _campus navigation gap_, _institutional assumption mismatch_.

==== Finders (Students / Staff Who Discover Items)

*Student (Daniel, TA)*:

“I found a phone outside the physics building.  
I didn’t want to just leave it there.  
But I also didn’t want to give it to the wrong person.”

- -> Observation: Ethical hesitation and ownership uncertainty.
- -> Derived concepts: _finder responsibility_, _ownership doubt_, _misdelivery fear_, _verification hesitation_, _custodial ambiguity_.


*Library Employee (Camila, part-time worker)*:

“Students bring water bottles, chargers, USB drives, everything.  
We just put them in a drawer with sticky notes.  
Sometimes people argue over who it belongs to.”

- -> Observation: Manual storage and informal labeling.
- -> Derived concepts: _manual logging_, _physical clutter accumulation_, _informal tagging_, _ownership dispute_, _storage overflow_.


*Campus Security Staff (informal interview note)*:

“We keep items in the office for a few weeks.  
If nobody claims them, they get discarded or stored somewhere else.  
There’s no consistent tracking system.”

- -> Observation: Time-based retention without structured tracking.
- -> Derived concepts: _retention window_, _unclaimed disposal_, _record absence_, _lifecycle inconsistency_, _institutional variance_.


*Student (heard in lab)*:

“I didn’t report the calculator I found.  
I just left it on the desk where I found it.”

- -> Observation: Non-reporting behavior due to low perceived value or effort.
- -> Derived concepts: _non-reporting tendency_, _effort aversion_, _value-based prioritization_, _passive return attempt_, _engagement barrier_.

==== Physical Environment (Campus Context)

*Observation (classroom building, evening)*:

Multiple empty classrooms with unattended items (notebooks, bottles, jackets).  
No signage indicating what to do if an item is found.

- -> Derived concepts: _unattended object frequency_, _instruction absence_, _behavioral ambiguity_, _environmental normalcy of loss_.


*Observation (library third floor office)*:

Drawer labeled “Lost & Found” contains mixed items with handwritten notes.  
No consistent format for notes (some have dates, some don’t).

- -> Derived concepts: _metadata inconsistency_, _informal documentation_, _date omission_, _traceability gap_, _storage heterogeneity_.


*Observation (parking lot)*:

Keys found near vehicles.  
High traffic area, low traceability.

- -> Derived concepts: _high-mobility zone_, _low-identifiability object_, _spatial anonymity_, _retrieval difficulty_, _public exposure risk_.

[discrete]
==== Behavioral Patterns Around Loss

*Observation (multiple interviews)*:

People often retrace steps physically before asking anyone.  
Only after failing do they ask peers or offices.

- -> Derived concepts: _physical retracing priority_, _self-recovery attempt_, _delayed reporting_, _effort escalation_, _search fatigue_.


*Student Comment (hallway conversation)*:

“I assumed someone would steal it if I didn’t find it quickly.”

- -> Observation: Perceived theft risk influences urgency.
- -> Derived concepts: _theft anxiety_, _urgency escalation_, _trust deficit_, _loss finality assumption_, _campus risk perception_.


*Repeated Theme (brainstorming session note)*:

- Items frequently lost: keys, ID cards, chargers, water bottles, USB drives, headphones.
- High-value electronics trigger faster reporting.
- Low-value items often abandoned.
- No unified visibility across departments.

- -> Derived concepts: _value-based reporting threshold_, _object category frequency_, _departmental isolation_, _visibility fragmentation_, _reporting asymmetry_.

==== 2.1.1.1 Requirements Acquisition (Questionnaire) and Elicitation Summary
:author: Anthony H Martinez-Gomez

==== Questionnaire (Acquisition)

The following questionnaire was used to gather input about lost-and-found experiences and to support evidence-based domain claims.

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| ID | Question

| Q1 | In the last *6 months*, how many times have you lost an item on campus? (0 / 1 / 2–3 / 4+)
| Q2 | What types of items did you lose? (keys / ID / earbuds / bottle / charger / laptop / other: ___)
| Q3 | When you realize you lost something, what is the *first* thing you usually do? (retrace steps / ask friends / check office / group chat / other)
| Q4 | In your last loss, which places/offices did you check? (library / security / department office / student affairs / cafeteria / other: ___)
| Q5 | About how many places did you check before you found it or gave up? (1 / 2 / 3 / 4+)
| Q6 | How long after losing something do you usually notice it is missing? (immediately / hours later / next day / other)
| Q7 | If you have found an item on campus, what did you do? (left it / turned in to office / posted in chat / kept it / other)
| Q8 | If you turned in a found item, where did you take it? (library / security / department office / student affairs / other)
| Q9 | What details help you identify your item *without* making it easy to fake-claim it? (open response)
| Q10 | What details would you *not* feel comfortable sharing publicly? (name / phone / ID details / exact location / photos / other)
| Q11 | Have you ever seen/heard of someone falsely claiming an item? (Yes/No). If yes, describe briefly.
| Q12 | What is the biggest obstacle in the current lost-and-found process at UPRM? (open response)
| Q13 | If you could change one thing about how lost & found works on campus, what would it be? (open response)
|===

==== Elicitation Summary (Preliminary Findings)

* Respondents: N = 10
* Stakeholder mix: 8 students, 2 staff (security)
* Collection method: Google Form
* Dates: March 16, 2026 to April 3, 2026
** Small sample size
** Limited representation of staff perspectives

Findings:

- 8/10 respondents reported losing at least one item in the last 6 months.
- Common items mentioned included water bottles, chargers, earbuds, and keys.
- Recovery attempts commonly involved checking multiple places (median = 2), especially the library, department offices, and group chats.
- Privacy concerns were common (avoiding phone/contact info, ID details, exact locations).
- 3/10 respondents mentioned concerns about suspicious or false claiming behavior.
- Most respondents reported noticing the loss one hour later.
