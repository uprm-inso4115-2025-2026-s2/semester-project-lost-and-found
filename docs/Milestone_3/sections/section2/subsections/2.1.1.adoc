===== Evidence add on (questionnaire summary)

* Respondents: N = 10 (8 students, 2 security staff)
* Collection method: Google Form
* Dates: March 16, 2026 to April 3, 2026
* Limitations:
  ** Small sample size
  ** Convenience sample (mostly within our network)
  ** Limited representation of staff perspectives

Key results:

* 8/10 reported losing at least one item in the last 6 months.
* Common items mentioned: water bottles, chargers, earbuds, keys.
* Median number of places checked before finding or giving up: 2.
* Places and channels commonly checked: library, department offices, group chats.
* Privacy boundaries were common (contact info, ID details, exact locations).
* 3/10 mentioned concerns about suspicious or false claiming behavior.
* Most respondents reported noticing the loss later the same day, often within a few hours.

===== Derived concepts (traceable back to raw observations)

The concepts below are derived from the raw observation table above. Each concept includes the observation numbers it is based on so that later concept analysis can be traced back to specific domain evidence.

[cols="2,4,2",options="header"]
|===
| Derived concept | What it means | Supported by obs #

| Delayed realization
| People often notice loss later, sometimes hours later or after offices/classrooms may be closed.
| 1, 3, 16, 17

| No clear starting point
| After noticing a loss, people are unsure which office, building, desk, or channel is the right first stop.
| 2, 5, 9, 14

| Place to place effort
| Recovery attempts require checking multiple places, routes, offices, and informal communication channels.
| 2, 3, 14, 16

| Fragmented channels
| People use informal channels such as social media and group chats, but these do not preserve consistent context or searchable records.
| 1, 4, 13

| Weak matching / false positives
| Informal matching creates false positives because descriptions may be incomplete or similar items may exist.
| 4, 13

| Finder hesitation
| Finders want to help but may not know whether to leave the item, turn it in, or avoid giving it to the wrong person.
| 6, 9

| Manual custody
| Items are stored in offices, drawers, or desks with inconsistent metadata and limited searchability.
| 7, 8, 11, 14

| Disputes at pickup
| Weak verification and incomplete item records can lead to disagreements about ownership.
| 7

| Retention window
| Items may be held temporarily and later discarded, moved, or stored elsewhere without consistent tracking.
| 8

| Non reporting / passive return
| Some finders do not report items and instead leave them where they were found.
| 9, 10

| Privacy boundary
| People avoid exposing personal contact information or sensitive item details publicly.
| 3, 12, 15

| Theft anxiety / urgency escalation
| Perceived risk of theft or misuse increases urgency and changes user behavior.
| 3, 6, 12, 17
