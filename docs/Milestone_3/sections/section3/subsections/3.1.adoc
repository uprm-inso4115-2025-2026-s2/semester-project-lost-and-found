== *Analytic Part*

=== *Concept Analysis*

This section refines the raw material from the descriptive part into a structured set of domain concepts, abstractions, and relationships. The process began by reviewing unprocessed notes such as “students post lost items,” “admins verify suspicious reports,” “images must be uploaded,” and “users should only edit their own reports.” Recurring patterns were abstracted into domain-level concepts that clarify structure and responsibility.

The result is a shared vocabulary that supports requirements engineering, architecture decisions (React + Supabase), and future evolution of the system.

==== Method

We systematically reviewed raw descriptions from Section 2, including:

* Informal interview fragments (e.g., “I lost my AirPods in the library,” “People post fake found items”).
* Brainstormed terminology (lost item, found item, claimant, verification, report status).
* Early UI sketches (form submission, image upload, dashboard view).
* Functional expectations (authentication required, ownership enforcement, admin moderation).

Each recurring entity, event, or behavior was evaluated to determine whether it represents:

* A persistent domain entity,
* A domain event,
* A lifecycle state,
* A system behavior or policy.

Ambiguous terms were clarified and generalized where possible to ensure consistency across the team.

==== Key Concepts Identified

*Item*:
Any physical object that can be lost or found.

*Report*:
 A representation of an occurrence in which a person communicates that an item was either lost or found. This communication may take different forms, such as a note, a bulletin board post or a digital entry. It captures essential information about the event such as title, description, category, location, timestamp, and images.

The report is distinct from the item itself; the item exists in the real world, while the report is its system representation.

*People roles*:

*Finder*: person who found the item.
*Owner*: person who lost the item.
*Holder*: person responsible for keeping found items.

The owner, finder and holder could be students, community member

[.modified]* Administrator: moderation authority.

*Ownership*:
Relationship between a report and the person who created it. It identifies who is responsible for the report on the domain.

*Image Asset*:
Raw notes frequently mentioned uploading photos separately from form data. This led to the abstraction of an *Image Asset* as a distinct concept stored in object storage.

Images are associated with reports but managed independently at the infrastructure level.

*Report Type*:
Distinguishes reports as either “lost” or “found”. This distinction reflects important semantic differences in the domain: 

* *Lost Item report*: typically submitted by someone who lost the item
* *Found Item report*: typically submitted by someone who found the item

Although both report types share common description elements(such as title, description, category), they differ in the type and reliability of available information. A finder typically knows where the item was found and can often provide images, while an owner may not know the exact location or be able to provide visual evidence.

Despite the differences, both are modeled under a single Report structure to maintain conceptual unity, while allowing certain attributes such as location or images to be optional.

*Status / Lifecycle State*:

Reports evolve over time. A report begins as active and may transition to states such as:

* `resolved`
* `archived`
* `removed`

This clarifies the changes in the outcome of the reported occurrence.

*Claim / Recovery Event*:
Some narratives included situations such as "I contacted the person and got my item back." This was abstracted as a *Recovery Event*, representing the successful return of an item through coordination between the involved parties (e.g. finder and owner)
It reflects the resolution of the occurrence associated with a report.

*Moderation*:
Concerns about spam or misleading reports were generalized into the concept of *Moderation*, a privileged capability assigned to administrators. Moderation affects report lifecycle and visibility.

==== Clarifications and Resolutions

* “Lost item” and “Found item” are not separate entities; both are modeled as *Report* instances with different types.
* “Post” and “Report” are treated synonymously, but “Report” is adopted as the formal domain term.
* “Delete” is distinguished from “Archive.” Deletion represents removing a report from consideration(no longer active or visible). Permanent deletion is not essential in this domain. Archiving retains it for reference while marking it as no longer active. This preserves historical information and avoids losing potentially relevant data.


These clarifications prevent semantic confusion in later stages of analysis and development.

==== Relationships and Abstractions:

* A *Person* can create one or more *Reports*.
* Each *Report* is associated with exactly one *Person*
* A *Report* may have multiple *Image Assets*.
* A *Report* has one *Report Type* (`lost` or `found`).
* A *Report* has one *Lifecycle State* at any time.
* An *Administrator* can modify lifecycle states of any report.
* A *Recovery Event* transitions a report from `"active"` to `"resolved"`.

These relationships formalize previously informal interactions.

==== Reasoning and Process (Domain Level)

This concept analysis is grounded in observations from the descriptive part such as narratives of lost and found items:

* Recurring mentions of reporting occurrences and coordinating recovery, led to formalizing *Reports*,*Image Assets* and *Claim/Recovery Event*
* Concerns about inappropriate or outdated posts led to modeling *Lifecycle State* explicitly  for reports,reflecting that reports evolve over time.
* Differentiating lost items reports from found items reports, highlighted the importance of *Report Type* and semantic distinctions
* Roles mentioned in narratives(e.g. holder, person who lost an item, person who found an item), clarified *People Roles* and their responsibilities

Through this analysis, the *domain understanding* is validated by confirming that all key entities and events, roles and their relationships can realistically be captured.

==== Requirements and Domain Model Inspection

To improve consistency, correctness, and traceability, the project conducted a structured inspection of domain and requirements artifacts.

===== Inspection Methodology

The inspection process followed two complementary strategies:

* *Defect-driven inspection*: focused on identifying problems such as missing concepts, contradictory assumptions, ambiguous terminology, incomplete relationships, and inconsistencies between sections.
* *Quality-driven inspection*: focused on evaluating clarity, completeness, traceability, realism, and alignment with the domain narrative.

The inspection was conducted iteratively, meaning findings from one round informed revisions and additional review cycles.

===== Inspectors and Responsibilities

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Inspector Role | Responsibility

| *Documentation Team Members*
| Reviewed terminology, narrative consistency, traceability between sections, and clarity of requirements.

| *Development Team Representatives*
| Reviewed feasibility, consistency of domain assumptions, and alignment between concepts and expected functionality.

| *Project Managers*
| Oversaw inspection progress, coordinated revisions, and ensured findings were addressed consistently across documentation.
|===

===== Inspected Artifacts

The following project artifacts were inspected:

* Terminology
* Narrative
* Domain concepts and abstractions from the descriptive part
* Requirements and stakeholder-driven goals
* Personas and user stories
* Relationships and lifecycle definitions
* Scope and synopsis consistency

===== Question Catalog

The following guiding questions were used during inspection:

* Does this concept already exist in the real-world domain, or is it introduced by a proposed solution?
* Can this term be understood independently of implementation details?
* Are stakeholders and their responsibilities represented realistically?
* Are all relevant events in the lost-and-found lifecycle represented?
* Are relationships between concepts explicit and non-contradictory?
* Does the narrative align with terminology definitions?
* Can requirements be traced back to domain observations or stakeholder needs?
* Are assumptions documented and justified?
* Does any section imply a workflow that depends on the future system rather than existing domain behavior?
* Are lifecycle transitions complete and logically consistent?

===== Inspection Findings and Revisions

The inspection identified several improvements required for consistency and completeness:

* Missing domain entities, including *Custody Location*, *Loss Location*, and *Holding Period*, were introduced into *Terminology Section*.
* Domain events such as *Item Handed Over* and *Holding Period Expired* were added to improve lifecycle representation.
* Narrative wording that implied the existence of a coordinated digital process was revised to instead describe observable communication channels and their effectiveness.
* Requirements and terminology were revised to improve traceability and reduce overlap between domain concepts and implementation concerns.
* Several concepts were relabeled to reflect the phase in which they are introduced rather than where they are used most frequently.

The inspection process improved the internal consistency of the project documentation and strengthened alignment between domain understanding, requirements engineering, and later design decisions.
