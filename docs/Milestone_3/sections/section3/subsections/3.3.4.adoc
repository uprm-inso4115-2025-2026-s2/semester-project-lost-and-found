
==== *Requirements Acquisition Resources and Documentation*

[discrete]
==== Requirements Acquisition Questionnaire

To acquire evidence for our domain claims, we designed and distributed a questionnaire to collect data about the lost-and-found experience on campus. This process involved both the creation of targeted questions and the collection of responses from relevant stakeholders. The goal was to gather concrete data that could inform our requirements decisions and support our claims about user needs and behaviors.

This section separates:

* *Acquisition* (the questions and data collection method)
* *Elicitation* (the interpretation of the collected responses)

===== Requirements Acquisition (Questionnaire)

====== Purpose

The questionnaire gathers evidence about:

- How often items are lost/found on campus (in the respondents’ experience)

- What recovery steps people currently take (offices/places checked)

- What information people are willing to share publicly

- What risks people perceive (false claims, privacy concerns)

- Which improvements would make recovery easier

====== Method (Acquisition)

- Collection method: Google Form (recommended) or direct messages.

- Target stakeholders: students (lost-item owners + finders) and optionally staff who handle found items.

- Time window: Mar/28/2026 - Apr/5/2026

- Respondents: N = 10

====== Questionnaire

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| ID | Question

| Q1 | In the last *6 months*, how many times have you lost an item on campus? `(0 / 1 / 2–3 / 4+)`
| Q2 | What items did you lose? `(keys / ID / earbuds / bottle / charger / laptop / other: ___)`
| Q3 | When you realize you lost something, what is the *first* thing you do? `(retrace steps / ask friends / check office / group chat / other)`
| Q4 | In your last loss, which places/offices did you check? `(library / security / department office / student affairs / cafeteria / other: ___)`
| Q5 | About how many places did you check before you found it or gave up? `(1 / 2 / 3 / 4+)`
| Q6 | How long after losing something do you usually notice it’s missing? `(immediately / hours later / next day / other)`
| Q7 | If you have found an item on campus, what did you do? `(left it / turned in to office / posted in chat / kept it / other)`
| Q8 | If you turned in a found item, where did you take it? `(library / security / department office / student affairs / other)`
| Q9 | What details help you identify your item *without* making it easy to fake-claim it? `(open response)`
| Q10 | What details would you *not* share publicly? `(name / phone / ID details / exact location / photos / other)`
| Q11 | Have you ever seen/heard of someone falsely claiming an item? `(Yes/No)`. If yes, describe briefly.
| Q12 | What is the biggest obstacle in the current lost-and-found process at UPRM? `(open response)`
| Q13 | If you could change one thing about how lost & found works on campus, what would it be? `(open response)`
|===


===== Elicitation Summary 

====== Method 

* Respondents: N = 10
* Stakeholder mix: 8 students, 2 staff (security)
* Collection method: Google Form 
* Dates: March 16, 2026 to April 3, 2026
** Small sample size
** Limited representation of staff perspectives

====== Findings 

* F1 (Frequency): 8/10 respondents reported losing at least one item in the last 6 months.
* F2 (Most common items): Top items mentioned were: 1) water bottle 2) charger 3) earbuds (tied with keys)
* F3 (Time-to-realization): Most respondents noticed loss: hours later.
* F4 (Recovery effort): Median number of places checked before finding/giving up: 2.
* F5 (Where people check): Most commonly checked places were: library, department offices, and group chats.
* F6 (Finder behavior): 10/10 respondents reported finding an item at least once; common actions were: turning it in to an office (most common), posting in a chat, or leaving it where it was.
* F7 (Privacy/trust): Common details people avoid posting publicly: phone/contact info, ID details, and exact locations (also serial numbers/photos of IDs); 5 respondents mentioned false-claim concerns.

====== What These Findings Support

Based on the findings above, we can support the following claims:

* C1: Lost items occur often enough (in our sample) to justify improving the recovery process.
* C2: Recovery currently requires checking multiple places/channels (place-to-place effort).
* C3: Privacy concerns exist and influence what information people are willing to share publicly.
* C4: Finder behavior is inconsistent, suggesting the need for clearer guidance and safer handoff/verification.
* C5: False claims are a concern for some users, indicating the need for robust verification and trust-building features in the system.

==== *Requirement Acquisition Techniques and Stakeholder Context Expansion*
:author: Tomas Esteban Gomez Mojica

[discrete]
==== Purpose

While the questionnaire provided quantitative and open-ended evidence regarding lost-and-found experiences at UPRM, additional acquisition techniques can provide deeper contextual understanding about stakeholder behavior, workflows, and operational constraints.

These techniques complement the questionnaire findings by helping explain *why* certain problems occur and how stakeholders interact with the current recovery process.

[discrete]
==== Stakeholder Interviews

===== Purpose

Stakeholder interviews allow developers to gather more detailed information regarding user experiences, frustrations, and expectations related to the lost-and-found process.

Unlike questionnaires, interviews support follow-up questions and clarification, making them useful for understanding operational and trust-related concerns in greater depth.

===== Target Stakeholders

- Students who have lost items
- Students who have found items
- Administrative staff handling found items
- Security personnel

===== Example Information Gathered

Interviews may help uncover:

- Difficulties when searching across multiple campus locations
- Concerns regarding privacy and false claims
- Current verification procedures
- Administrative challenges in managing reports
- Expectations regarding notifications and report tracking

===== Example Findings

Example interview findings may include:

- Students are unsure which office should be checked first after losing an item.
- Administrative custodians spend significant time responding to repeated status inquiries.
- Users are uncomfortable sharing personal contact information publicly.
- Staff members are concerned about fraudulent recovery attempts.

===== Example Requirements Supported

The findings above support requirements such as:

- The system shall centralize lost-and-found reporting.
- Users shall receive updates regarding report status.
- The system shall support private communication mechanisms.
- Administrative custodians shall verify ownership before approving recovery.

[discrete]
==== Observation of Existing Campus Processes

===== Purpose

Observation focuses on studying how lost-and-found activities currently occur across campus environments. This technique helps identify workflow inefficiencies and operational patterns that stakeholders may not explicitly describe during interviews or questionnaires.

===== Observation Areas

Observation may focus on:

- How found items are submitted
- How offices record information
- How students attempt recovery
- How ownership verification occurs
- How communication is handled between stakeholders

===== Example Findings

Observation may reveal that:

- Different campus departments maintain disconnected records.
- Students often check multiple offices before locating information.
- Administrative staff manually track reports using paper or spreadsheets.
- Some items remain unclaimed due to lack of centralized visibility.

===== Example Requirements Supported

These findings support requirements such as:

- The system shall maintain a centralized repository of reports.
- Administrative custodians shall access shared report dashboards.
- Reports shall support status tracking.
- The system shall maintain recovery history records.

[discrete]
==== User Stories and Scenario Discussions

===== Purpose

User stories and scenario discussions help connect stakeholder goals with realistic campus situations. These techniques focus on understanding stakeholder motivations and expected interactions with the system.

===== Example User Stories

*US5:* As a Student Claimant, I want to receive updates regarding my report so that I know whether my item has been found.

*US6:* As a Finder, I want to quickly submit information about a discovered item so that it can be returned efficiently.

*US7:* As an Administrative Custodian, I want to verify ownership before approving recovery so that fraudulent claims are prevented.

===== Example Scenario

A student notices several hours later that a calculator was left behind after class. The student checks nearby offices and group chats before submitting a lost-item report.

Meanwhile, another student submits a found-item report describing a calculator discovered in the same building.

An administrative custodian reviews both reports, compares identifying details, and verifies ownership before approving recovery.

===== Example Requirements Supported

This scenario supports requirements such as:

- Users shall submit lost-item and found-item reports digitally.
- Reports shall support item descriptions and image uploads.
- The system shall notify users regarding potential matches.
- Administrative custodians shall validate ownership claims before release approval.

[discrete]
==== Relationship to Questionnaire Findings

These acquisition techniques complement the questionnaire findings described previously.

For example:

- Questionnaire findings showed that students often check multiple locations before recovering an item.
- Observation and interviews help explain that reporting processes are currently fragmented across departments.

Similarly:

- Questionnaire findings identified privacy concerns and fear of false claims.
- Interviews and scenario discussions help determine what verification and privacy mechanisms stakeholders expect from the system.

Together, these acquisition techniques strengthen the connection between stakeholder evidence and future requirements specification.
