
==== *Risk Mitigation and Countermeasures*

Each countermeasure is justified based on its expected effectiveness in reducing the likelihood or impact of the associated risk while considering system usability and stakeholder needs. The following table summarizes the candidate countermeasures for each identified risk, along with their justification and expected outcomes.

[cols="1,3,3,4,4", options="header"]
|===
| Risk ID | Candidate Countermeasure | Risk Reduction Tactic | Justification / Discussion | Expected Outcome

| <<RSK-01>>
| Enforce structured reporting forms with required descriptive attributes
| Prevention
| Structured inputs reduce ambiguity and increase match reliability
| Higher matching accuracy; fewer false positives; improved user trust

| <<RSK-01>>
| Validate user inputs before submission
| Detection
| Ensures missing or inconsistent information is flagged early
| Reduces likelihood of invalid reports; encourages complete information

| <<RSK-01>>
| Provide optional guidance and templates for item description
| Mitigation
| Helps users provide sufficient detail without overwhelming them
| Improves quality of reports; reduces errors and follow-up communication

| <<RSK-02>>
| Require explicit justification for administrative overrides
| Prevention
| Reduces likelihood of unnecessary or arbitrary state changes
| Maintains lifecycle integrity; auditability preserved

| <<RSK-02>>
| Record all administrative actions in audit logs
| Detection
| Allows monitoring and verification of all interventions
| Traceable actions; deters improper modifications

| <<RSK-02>>
| Notify report owners when overrides occur
| Mitigation
| Keeps stakeholders informed and allows corrective action if needed
| Increased transparency; user trust maintained

| <<RSK-03>>
| Enforce strict separation of public and private attributes
| Prevention
| Limits exposure of sensitive information to unauthorized actors
| Privacy maintained; reduces fraudulent claims

| <<RSK-03>>
| Restrict access to verification information to authorized personnel
| Prevention
| Ensures that only validated actors can access sensitive data
| Ownership verification remains secure; reduces misuse

| <<RSK-03>>
| Validate input fields for potential private information before submission
| Detection
| Detects accidental disclosure of sensitive details in public fields
| Users are alerted; prevents privacy violations
|===

===== Countermeasure Evaluation Criteria

The effectiveness of each countermeasure should be evaluated to determine whether the associated risk has been reduced to an acceptable level.

Evaluation criteria provide a structured method for assessing whether implemented countermeasures achieve their intended objectives while preserving usability and stakeholder expectations.

[cols="2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Countermeasure Category | Evaluation Criteria

| Structured reporting forms
| Percentage of submitted reports containing complete required information

| Input validation
| Number of invalid submissions detected before system acceptance

| Reporting guidance and templates
| Reduction in incomplete or ambiguous item descriptions

| Administrative override controls
| Percentage of overrides accompanied by documented justification

| Audit logging
| Ability to reconstruct and verify administrative actions

| User notifications
| Successful delivery of notifications related to administrative actions

| Privacy controls
| Number of unauthorized disclosures of sensitive information

| Access restrictions
| Verification that sensitive information is accessible only to authorized actors
|===

===== Evaluation Considerations

Countermeasure effectiveness should be reviewed periodically throughout project development and operation.

When evaluation results indicate that a countermeasure is not sufficiently reducing risk, additional controls or revised mitigation strategies may be required.

This evaluation process supports continuous improvement of system reliability, security, privacy protection, and stakeholder trust.
