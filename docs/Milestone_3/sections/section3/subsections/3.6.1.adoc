=== *Requirements Prioritization and Decision Analysis*


==== *Evaluation Criteria Definition*

This section defines the evaluation criteria used to prioritize system features. The goal is to establish a clear and justified basis for comparing requirements before applying decision-making techniques.

===== Criteria Selection

The prioritization process is based on two primary evaluation criteria:

- *Value*: Represents the contribution of a feature toward achieving the system’s primary objective, which is to maximize successful item recovery and improve user interaction within the Lost & Found platform.
- *Cost*: Represents the estimated implementation effort required for each feature, including development complexity, integration difficulty, and maintenance overhead.

===== Criteria Justification

These criteria were selected through team discussion and consensus. Each team member proposed and evaluated potential criteria, including usability, security, scalability, and reliability.

To formalize the decision, team members independently identified the criteria they considered most relevant for prioritization. The results were consolidated as follows:

After discussion, the team agreed that:

- *Value* captures the functional importance and user impact of each feature.
- *Cost* captures the feasibility and resource constraints associated with implementation.

Other criteria were considered but excluded to avoid overcomplicating the decision model. The team concluded that Value and Cost provide a balanced and sufficient framework for early-stage prioritization.

===== Evaluation Scale

To ensure consistent comparisons, the team adopted the standard AHP pairwise comparison scale:

- 1 – Equal contribution or cost  
- 3 – Slightly more  
- 5 – Strongly more  
- 7 – Very strongly more  
- 9 – Extremely more  

Intermediate values (2, 4, 6, 8) may be used when necessary.

This scale allows team members to express relative importance in a structured and quantifiable way, forming the basis for the prioritization process described in the next section.

===== Decision Interpretation Guidelines

The prioritization results should be interpreted using consistent decision rules so that requirement rankings remain understandable and justified.

The following guidelines support interpretation of Value and Cost comparisons:

* High-value, low-cost requirements should generally receive the highest priority.
* High-value, high-cost requirements should be evaluated against available resources and project deadlines.
* Low-value, low-cost requirements may be considered if they support usability or reduce future rework.
* Low-value, high-cost requirements should generally be deferred unless they are required for compliance, safety, or core system operation.

===== Sensitivity Considerations

The team recognizes that prioritization outcomes may change if the relative importance of Value and Cost changes.

For example:

* Increasing the weight of Value may favor user-facing features that directly improve item recovery.
* Increasing the weight of Cost may favor simpler features that can be implemented quickly.
* Equal weighting may provide a balanced view between usefulness and feasibility.

Sensitivity analysis helps determine whether prioritization decisions remain stable under different assumptions.

===== Tie-Breaking Rules

When two requirements receive similar prioritization results, the team should apply tie-breaking rules.

Tie-breaking considerations include:

* direct contribution to successful item recovery
* impact on user trust and safety
* dependency on other features
* implementation risk
* relevance to the current milestone

These rules help prevent arbitrary prioritization decisions and support more transparent requirement selection.

===== Summary

Adding decision guidelines and sensitivity considerations improves the requirements prioritization process by making the interpretation of Value and Cost comparisons more explicit.

This supports more consistent decision-making and helps the team justify why certain requirements should be implemented earlier than others.
