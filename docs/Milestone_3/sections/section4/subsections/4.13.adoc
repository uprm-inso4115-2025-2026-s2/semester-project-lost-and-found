===== Introduction

Modern systems are increasingly socio-technical, meaning they combine technology, human interaction, and organizational processes.

Goal-Oriented Requirements Engineering (GORE) focuses on understanding why a system is needed before deciding how it should be built. One of the main frameworks used in GORE is iStar 2.0, which helps developers analyze stakeholder goals, tasks, resources, and dependencies.

A Lost & Found platform at UPRM could benefit from these concepts by improving organization, communication, and user experience.

===== What is GORE?

Goal-Oriented Requirements Engineering (GORE) is a requirements engineering approach focused on identifying stakeholder goals and system motivations.

Instead of only focusing on technical specifications, GORE analyzes:

* User objectives
* Stakeholder interactions
* System purpose
* Decision-making processes

Benefits of GORE include improved communication, better planning, easier identification of requirements, and stronger decision-making.

===== Socio-Technical Systems

A socio-technical system combines technology with human interaction.

Examples include:

* Healthcare systems
* Online shopping platforms
* University management systems
* Lost & Found platforms

The Lost & Found platform is considered socio-technical because it depends on students, administrators, and digital communication working together effectively.

===== What is iStar 2.0?

iStar 2.0 is a goal-modeling framework used in Requirements Engineering to represent stakeholder intentions and dependencies.

It helps analysts understand:

* Who uses the system
* What goals stakeholders have
* How actors interact
* Which resources are necessary
* What dependencies exist

===== Main Concepts of iStar 2.0

Actors:: Entities that participate in the system.

Goals:: Objectives actors want to achieve.

Tasks:: Actions required to achieve goals.

Resources:: Informational or physical elements needed for tasks.

Qualities:: Desired characteristics such as security or usability.

Dependencies:: Relationships where one actor relies on another.

===== Applying iStar to the Lost & Found Platform

The Lost & Found platform includes several actors such as students, administrators, finders, and the notification system.

Students depend on the platform to report and recover items, while administrators depend on moderation tools to maintain safety and organization.

Modeling these relationships helps developers better understand system requirements before implementation.

===== Benefits of Goal Modeling

Goal modeling provides several benefits:

* Improves organization and communication
* Helps identify system requirements early
* Supports better decision-making during planning
* Creates a more user-centered system design

===== Conclusion

GORE and iStar 2.0 provide powerful tools for understanding socio-technical systems.

Applying these concepts to a Lost & Found platform can improve communication, organization, reliability, and user experience.

Overall, goal modeling supports better system planning and development.

==== [Lecture Topic Task]: Goal Refinement and Goal Diagrams
:author: Victoria Miranda Osuna

The goal is to analyze how goal refinement and goal diagrams can organize and model 
the objectives of a Lost & Found platform while maintaining consistency between high 
level stakeholder goals and lower level operational goals. High level objectives 
include successfully recovering lost items, improving communication between users, 
maintaining secure claim verification, and increasing platform reliability and trust. 
These objectives can be represented through goal diagrams to show how different system 
functions contribute to the overall platform purpose.

Lower level goals such as item reporting, notifications, claim verification, and search
 filtering support the platform’s main objectives. Accurate item reporting improves 
 matching between lost and found items, while notifications keep users informed about recovery 
 progress. Claim verification reduces false ownership claims, and search filters improve 
 recovery efficiency. The analysis also examines conflicts between usability, security, and 
 recovery efficiency goals. For example, faster recovery processes may reduce verification 
 accuracy, while stronger verification procedures can slow recovery times. Simplified reporting
  forms may collect insufficient information, and excessive notifications may overwhelm users.

Why and how questioning techniques are used to refine broad platform goals into smaller 
assignable sub goals. Questions such as “Why recover items quickly?” lead to goals focused on 
improving user satisfaction, while “How to recover items quickly?” results in solutions like 
automated notifications. Similarly, verifying ownership helps prevent fraudulent claims, and 
requiring item descriptions or proof images supports that goal. Success is achieved when goal
refinements clearly show relationships between high level and low level objectives, goal 
diagrams demonstrate alternative refinement paths, conflicts between goals are documented, 
and the refined sub goals contribute to effective item recovery and a better user experience.
