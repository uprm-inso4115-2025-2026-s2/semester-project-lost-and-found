Human Agents: 

- Students and Faculty (UPRM): Report lost/ found items

- Manage Reports (Lost or Found) Agent

==== Modeling Biddable Agents and System Failure Scenarios
:author: Tasnim Said Khader

==== Introduction
The Lost & Found domain depends heavily on human participation. Unlike mechanical or deterministic actors, human participants are biddable agents: they can be directed or encouraged to perform actions, but their future behavior cannot be guaranteed.

Because of this uncertainty, the domain theory must explicitly account for situations where expected actions are delayed, incomplete, incorrect, excessive, or omitted entirely.

==== Identified Biddable Agents

The following agents were identified as biddable agents within the Lost & Found domain:

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Biddable Agent
|Expected Responsibilities
|Domain Functions / Behaviors

|Owner
|Recognize item loss, communicate identifying information, initiate recovery attempts, and participate in verification.
|Loss Realization, Claim Attempt, Claim Verification

|Finder
|Discover found items, communicate discovery, transfer custody, or attempt direct return.
|Item Found, Item Handed Over, Reporting

|Holder
|Maintain temporary custody, preserve availability of items, and facilitate retrieval.
|Custody Management, Holding Period

|Returner
|Verify legitimacy of claims and transfer belongings to rightful owners.
|Claim Verification, Item Returned

|Community Participant
|Communicate information that increases visibility of lost or found items.
|Communication, Matching
|===

==== Dishonored Expectations by Domain Function

The following table identifies situations in which biddable agents may fail to honor expectations, particularly regarding timing and correctness of actions.

[cols="1,2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Domain Function
|Expected Action
|Possible Dishonored Expectation
|Domain Impact

|Loss Realization
|Owner notices item loss quickly.
|Owner notices too late or forgets entirely.
|Reduced probability of successful retrieval due to delayed action.

|Reporting
|Finder or Owner communicates item information.
|Report is incomplete, inaccurate, delayed, or skipped entirely.
|Matching becomes less reliable or impossible.

|Item Handed Over
|Finder transfers item appropriately.
|Finder keeps item too long, gives it to the wrong person, or forgets.
|Item visibility decreases and custody becomes uncertain.

|Claim Verification
|Returner or Holder checks ownership indicators.
|Verification is performed incorrectly, rushed, or skipped.
|Risk of wrongful return or unresolved disputes.

|Custody Management
|Holder maintains temporary availability.
|Item becomes misplaced, inaccessible, or discarded prematurely.
|Recovery becomes impossible despite earlier success in matching.

|Communication
|Community participant shares relevant information.
|Incorrect, excessive, or misleading information is shared.
|Confusion increases and false matches may occur.
|===

==== Timing and Correctness Failure Scenarios
1. Scenario B1: Delayed Finder Action
    A Finder discovers a lost item but postpones informing others or transferring custody until much later.

* Expected behavior: the item becomes visible quickly through communication or transfer.
* Dishonored expectation: action occurs at the wrong time (late).
* Impact on domain theory: successful return depends not only on discovery, but also on timely communication. The domain must therefore recognize timing as a factor influencing Matching and return probability.

2. Scenario B2: Incorrect Claim Verification
    A Returner returns an item without sufficiently confirming ownership.

* Expected behavior: ownership indicators are checked before transfer.
* Dishonored expectation: action performed incorrectly.
* Impact on domain theory: recovery alone cannot be considered universally successful; legitimacy of recovery must also be represented. This reinforces the importance of Claim Verification.

3. Scenario B3: Partial Reporting
    An Owner or Finder communicates incomplete identifying details.

* Expected behavior: sufficient details are communicated for identification.
* Dishonored expectation: action performed partially.
* Impact on domain theory: incomplete information reduces confidence in Matching and may delay successful return.

4. Scenario B4: Communication Omission
    A Community Participant knows information relevant to an item but chooses not to communicate it.

* Expected behavior: information contributing to visibility is shared.
* Dishonored expectation: action skipped entirely.
* Impact on domain theory: item visibility decreases, limiting the probability of successful association between lost and found belongings.

==== Monitoring Unreliable Human Behavior

Because biddable agents cannot be fully controlled, the domain theory assumes that actions may deviate from expectations. Rather than assuming ideal behavior, the domain incorporates mechanisms for recognizing uncertainty:

- Multiple communication channels are considered valid because no single participant can be relied upon completely.
- Claim Verification exists to reduce consequences of incorrect or dishonest actions.
- Holding Periods account for delayed owner action and timing uncertainty.
- Temporary custody recognizes that responsibility may shift between agents.

These mechanisms do not eliminate unreliability but ensure that the domain remains meaningful even when participants deviate from expected behavior.

==== Narrative Update

The domain narrative recognizes that successful item return depends not only on domain events occurring, but also on when and how human agents perform them. A *Finder* may delay communication, an *Owner* may realize a loss too late, or a *Holder* may mishandle custody. As a result, the domain is modeled as a process influenced by human reliability, communication quality, timing, and judgment rather than as a guaranteed sequence of successful actions.
